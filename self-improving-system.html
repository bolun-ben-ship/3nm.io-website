How it actually works

How it gets smarter without you touching it.

For a claim like “it compounds overnight,” the how is the proof. Here’s the whole mechanism: three steps, nothing hidden.

  1. 1

    Reads across everything.

    It ingests your client history, past projects, playbooks, notes, decisions: plain files it can read end to end, not folders it dumps and forgets.

  2. 2

    Connects it overnight.

    While you sleep, it re-reads the whole base and looks for links between things filed months and clients apart. New knowledge you add tonight gets connected to everything already there.

  3. 3

    Surfaces what you’d have missed.

    In the morning you get the connections worth acting on: the “this client’s problem is the one we solved for someone else two years ago” moment, handed to you instead of lost.

A concrete one

On 12 June 2026, a prospect asked me a pricing question I was sure I’d never handled. Overnight, my own system connected it to a scoping call from November 2025 (a different industry, a different client, but the same underlying constraint) and surfaced the exact number I’d landed on back then and why. I hadn’t tagged either. I hadn’t linked them. I’d have spent an afternoon re-deriving it, or worse, guessed. The system had already done the work while I slept. That’s the entire product: not a place to store what you know, but something that keeps finding what you already knew and forgot.

Where does everything your firm knows actually live?

Seven years in, the answer is almost always the same, and it’s the answer that keeps you up at night.

It’s in your head. You’re the search engine. Every “how did we handle X” routes through you, and it always will until you write it all down, which you never will.

It’s in one other person’s head. If they take two weeks off (or leave), a chunk of how your firm actually works walks out the door with them.

You tried Notion. It rotted. It became another folder nobody updates, because keeping a wiki alive is a job, and nobody has that job.

The same problem gets solved from scratch, twice. Something you cracked for a client in 2024 gets re-figured-out in 2026 because nobody remembered it existed.

Your best judgment never gets written down. The pricing calls, the “don’t take this kind of client,” the workaround that saved a project. The most valuable knowledge you have is the least documented.

Questions

The things founders ask before they opt in.

Straight answers. If yours isn’t here, email ben@3nm.io.

Is my data safe?

Yes, and it never leaves your control. The system is built on plain files that sit in your own storage, on infrastructure you own. Nothing is pooled into a shared model, and nothing trains anyone else’s product on your firm’s knowledge. On delivery, the whole thing is yours to host wherever you want.

What if I’m not technical?

You don’t touch the code. I build it, hand it over documented, and it runs on its own overnight without you managing it. Using it is asking it questions in plain language. Notion asked you to become a librarian; this doesn’t.

Do I own it?

Completely. Every line of code, every file, the whole system becomes yours on delivery. No SaaS seat, no lock-in, no “cancel and lose it.” It’s an asset on your side of the table, and it appreciates as your firm adds to it.

How is this not just Notion?

Notion is a place you put things and then have to maintain. This reads across everything on its own and connects it overnight. The part where a human keeps it alive is exactly the part I removed. Notion stores. This one thinks about what you stored while you sleep.

What’s actually delivered?

The self-improving knowledge system built on your firm’s real data: the ingestion setup, the overnight connection engine, a plain-language way to query it, and the plain files underneath, all documented and handed over as yours.

How long does it take?

A working version on your real data early, then the full build inside a fixed window, same cadence as every build on my case-study page: scoped in writing within 48 hours, something real by day 7.
